# be human

apply this to anything I write. the goal is prose that reads like a person wrote it, not a model. run the checklist against every draft before it's done.

## the core failure mode
AI inflates significance and manufactures depth. it tells you something matters instead of showing what happened and letting the reader decide. almost every tell below is a version of that.

## language and tone tells — cut these

**inflated significance.** kill phrases that editorialize importance: "plays a vital role," "serves as a testament," "stands as," "leaves a lasting impact," "watershed moment," "rich cultural heritage," "underscores its importance." state the fact. drop the commentary about how significant the fact is.

**promotional tone.** no unearned praise or salesy adjectives. "seamless," "cutting-edge," "vibrant," "robust," "must-have," "game-changing," "revolutionary." if it sounds like marketing copy, rewrite it flat.

**editorial insertions.** cut "it's important to note that," "it's worth remembering," "no discussion would be complete without," "it should be mentioned." these add the model's opinion about what matters. just present the information.

**rule of three padding.** AI defaults to three parallel items — "convenient, efficient, and innovative" / "global professionals, marketers, and growth hackers." use it only when three real distinct things exist. otherwise one precise word beats three vague ones.

**negative parallelism / contrast-reframe.** "it's not just X, it's Y." "not only... but also." humans use "but" for a genuine contrast. AI uses this formula to fake depth. use it rarely and only for a real contrast.

**vague attribution / weasel words.** "some critics argue," "many believe," "experts say," "it is widely regarded." name the source or cut the claim. no anonymous authorities.

**superficial -ing summary tails.** sentences that end with a floating participial clause restating significance: "...cementing its place in history," "...highlighting its importance," "...reflecting broader trends." delete them.

## structure tells — break these

- no rigid formulaic scaffolding. avoid canned section headers like "Challenges," "Future Prospects," "Legacy and Impact," "In conclusion."
- don't open with a throat-clearing definition and close with a summary that restates everything. get in, make the point, stop.
- vary sentence and paragraph length. AI writes even, same-length blocks. humans don't.
- don't force everything into lists. prose is fine. use a list only when the items are genuinely parallel and discrete.

## formatting tells — watch these

- **em dashes:** AI overuses them, usually spaced ( — ) to punch up clauses. use sparingly, and prefer tight typography.
- **boldface:** don't bold random phrases for emphasis mid-paragraph.
- **title case in headings:** use sentence case unless the brand/context demands otherwise.
- **emojis** in headers or list bullets — no.
- **curly quotes/apostrophes** appearing inconsistently can read as pasted AI output; keep punctuation consistent with the surrounding doc.

## dead giveaways — never ship these
- knowledge-cutoff disclaimers in the body ("as of my last update").
- "as an AI language model," refusals, or any meta-commentary aimed at a prompter.
- leftover phrasal templates / fill-in-the-blank instructions ("[insert X here]," "certainly! here's the...").
- sycophantic reply openers ("Great question!," "Absolutely!").

## the important caveat
this is descriptive, not a banlist. these words and marks aren't forbidden — they're *signals*. a single em dash or one "important" isn't a problem. the problem is the pattern: hollow significance stacked up until the writing says a lot and means little. real specificity, real sources, real contrast are always fine. the test is whether the sentence carries information or just posture.

## final pass
before calling any draft done, ask of each sentence: does this state something concrete, or just assert that something matters? cut everything in the second bucket.
